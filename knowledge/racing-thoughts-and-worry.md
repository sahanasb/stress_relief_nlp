# Racing Thoughts And Worry

## When it helps
When the mind loops through what-ifs, catastrophic outcomes, or replays mistakes, simple thinking tools can create distance and perspective.

## Name it and ask what is in your control
Notice the worry and say, "I'm having the thought that..." which separates you from the thought. Then ask whether this is something you can act on now. If yes, choose one small step. If not, practise letting it sit without solving it.

## Check the thought
Ask what evidence supports the worry and what evidence goes against it. Ask what you would tell a friend with the same worry. Think about the most likely outcome, not only the worst one.

## Scheduled worry time
Set aside ten to fifteen minutes each day for worrying. When a worry appears at other times, jot it down and postpone it to that slot. Many worries lose their grip by then.
